# Writing Standards

Apply these to prose you author. Exact text comes first, meaning second, style last.

## Protect exact text

Keep source material byte-for-byte: user text, quotes, vendor output, code, identifiers, commands, flags, numbers, frontmatter, schemas, parser tokens, and templates. A style rule never applies inside them.

## Preserve meaning

Keep normative `must`, `shall`, and `should`, and keep `may`, `might`, or `could` where they carry permission or real uncertainty. Never make evidence sound stronger than it is. Never add hedges that the evidence does not need: state the claim you hold.

## Lead with the conclusion

Write for one busy reader who must decide or act. Put the recommendation, finding, or request in the first sentence, and support after it. Give the number, file, or measurement instead of a generality. Name what the reader does next. A consuming skill's format contract outranks this order.

## Cut stock AI prose

- Delete words that add no technical meaning: seamless, robust, holistic, transformative, powerful, cutting-edge, best-in-class, leverage, delve, crucial, pivotal.
- Rewrite vague metaphors such as "moves the needle", "surface area", "center of gravity", and "unlocks" as the concrete file, action, or effect.
- Delete "it is important to note", "simply", "just", "of course", and offers of further help.
- Do not force facts into groups of three or into "not only X, but also Y" frames.
- Use headings, lists, and bold only for real structure. No decorative emoji.
- No em dashes. Split the sentence or use a comma.

These words survive in quotes, identifiers, proper nouns, and terms of art where a substitute changes the meaning ("robust statistics").
