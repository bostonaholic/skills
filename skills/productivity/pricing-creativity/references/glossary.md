# Glossary

Key terms from Blair Enns's _Pricing Creativity_, defined in paraphrase. Chapter
numbers point to the chapter files.

## Value and price

- **Subjective theory of value**: value is a judgment made by whoever is doing
  the valuing, not a property of the thing, and this holds in
  business-to-business sales too (ch01).
- **Labor theory of value**: the belief that value equals the effort to produce
  something. Hourly billing descends from it, and it resurfaces under stress
  (ch01, ch24).
- **Value triad**: Macdivitt and Wilkinson's view that value is revenue gains
  plus cost reductions plus emotional contributions. Emotion magnifies the
  economic value, and risk mitigation is the strongest emotional contribution
  (ch01).
- **Fairness**: how a client feels about the price they pay. It is a feeling,
  not a calculation, and the client's to judge (ch01, ch24).
- **Double thank-you**: both parties sincerely thank each other at the close;
  the test of a maximal sustainable price (ch01).
- **Desired future state**: the client's organizational needs plus personal
  wants; what a value pricer sells and prices (ch03, ch09).
- **Inputs, outputs, or value**: the three things a firm can sell: hours,
  deliverables at a fixed price, or the client's desired future state (ch03).
- **Value chain**: Client, Value, Price, Cost, Solutions. Prices come before
  solutions, reversing the cost-based order of product, cost, then price that
  Nagle and Holden describe (ch03, ch07, ch09, ch10).
- **Cost ceiling**: the most worth spending to deliver at a given price, about
  60% of it (ch03, ch07).
- **Value drivers**: forms of value beyond the core service, such as speed,
  senior access, reporting, price certainty, payment terms, and guarantees. They
  are the building blocks of options (ch04).
- **Price certainty**: a fixed price that removes cost surprises. It earns a
  premium only when the firm actually carries the delivery risk (ch03, ch14,
  ch15).
- **Price integrity**: round, bundled prices with no visible formula. Strategic
  prices end in three zeros (ch04).
- **Price discrimination**: charging different clients different prices for
  similar work, according to their willingness to pay (ch05).

## Options

- **Anchor**: the highest-priced option, presented first. It exists to make the
  middle option look like good value, not to be bought (ch07, ch11).
- **Anchoring and adjusting**: people start from the first number they hear and
  adjust too little away from it, even when they know it is happening (ch07,
  ch18).
- **Anchoring against guaranteed value**: asking what the client would consider
  fair pay if the outcome were guaranteed, to set the top of the range ($Y)
  (ch09).
- **Money-no-object exercise**: building the anchor by asking what the firm
  would do, and charge, if the client had no budget limit (ch11).
- **Uncertainty discount**: the gap between the price of a guaranteed outcome
  and the price of the same work without a guarantee (ch09, ch11).
- **Extremeness aversion**: buyers avoid the highest and lowest options and
  drift toward the middle (ch06, ch07).
- **Decoy**: an option added to steer the buyer toward a different one (ch06).
- **Priming**: the first information received sets the reference point, which is
  why the anchor must come first (ch07).
- **Middle option**: the option designed to be chosen most often and to carry
  the highest margin (ch13).
- **Home Depot Option**: a middle option between a diagnostic and full
  implementation, in which the firm sets the strategy and helps the client's own
  team execute (ch13).
- **Help forms**: knowledge transfer, oversight, standards, reporting, and open
  access, unbundled and placed in the middle option and the anchor (ch13).
- **Low option**: the cheapest option, designed to stay profitable and
  low-effort even when a price buyer takes it (ch12).
- **Time block**: a fixed block of junior hours, paid in advance, with every
  other value driver removed. The engagement ends when the hours run out (ch12).
- **Charm pricing**: a price ending in 9. Use it only to nudge toward the middle
  option (ch18).
- **Choice architecture**: shaping a decision without taking away the freedom to
  choose (ch06, ch18).

## Buyers and conversations

- **Price buyer**: buys on price, treating the purchase as an expense and the
  firm as a vendor (ch12).
- **Value buyer**: does at least rough ROI math, treating the purchase as an
  investment (ch12).
- **Poker player**: a value buyer who bluffs price sensitivity to negotiate;
  Reed Holden's term (ch12).
- **Just Four Conversations**: the probative, qualifying, value, and closing
  conversations. Each has one objective, plus keeping the expert practitioner
  position (ch09).
- **The Flip**: the moment the client starts seeing the firm as an expert rather
  than a vendor. It gates the value conversation (ch09).
- **Three-year question**: Dan Sullivan's opening question, which asks the
  client to picture a happy meeting three years out and describe what happened
  (ch09).
- **Value conversation**: the conversation that commits the client to a desired
  future state, agrees on metrics and value, and gives pricing guidance (ch09).
- **Pricing guidance**: a spoken $X to $Y range, given before any price is
  written (ch08, ch09).
- **Rule of Money**: Enns's rule that those who avoid talking about money don't
  make it (ch08).
- **Magic vs. logic**: creative work that senior clients take part in vs.
  operational work they delegate; Charles Kirchner's distinction (ch09, ch23).
- **Performance risk**: doubt about the firm's ability to deliver; the main
  reason a price gets discounted (ch09, ch17).
- **Diagnostic**: a branded, paid discovery phase that ends in findings,
  recommendations, and a one-page proposal. It is how a firm gets paid to scope,
  and a natural low option (ch10, ch13).
- **Unpaid proposal**: one page of three or four option columns with prices at
  the bottom, and nothing else (ch10).
- **Agreement in principle**: the client picks an option, a price, and a start
  date before any contract is drafted (ch10).
- **Closing conversation**: the meeting where the client chooses an option, with
  every decision maker present. Presenting diagnostic findings counts as one
  (ch09, ch10).

## Risk and pricing models

- **Premium**: the amount above a benchmark price, understood as an insurance
  premium against risk the firm takes on (ch17).
- **Risk spectrum**: the range from selling capacity or time (the client's risk,
  lowest price) to guaranteed value (the firm's risk, highest price) (ch17).
- **Client compensation portfolio**: Ron Baker's mix of high- and low-risk
  engagements that averages out to the firm's risk tolerance (ch11).
- **Satisfaction guarantee**: David Maister's offer that an unsatisfied client
  may pay what they think the work was worth (ch16).
- **Tip clause**: Ron Baker's pre-agreed invitation for the client to pay more
  at the end if delighted (ch16).
- **Pay-upfront-with-a-guarantee**: the client pays for phase one in full and
  gets the money back if unhappy (ch20).
- **Retainer**: a fixed monthly fee, usually for 12 months, and almost always
  cost-based in practice (ch14).
- **Open access**: paid, client-initiated access to the firm's principals.
  Enns's floor was $5k a month (ch13, ch14).
- **Reserved capacity**: a retainer that holds implementation capacity for a
  client with large, sporadic needs (ch14).
- **Lease model**: recurring payment for use of a deliverable instead of a
  one-time sale; websites and apps are software. Licensing retained IP is a
  variant (ch15).
- **Financed amount**: a lease clause that makes front-loaded development cost
  due even if the client cancels (ch15).
- **MLOE**: minimum level of engagement, about 10% of the annual AGI target
  (ch05).
- **Customized vs. productized firm**: bespoke, consultative work vs.
  standardized, scalable offerings. Only a productized firm publishes package
  prices (ch05, ch14).

## Discounts and negotiation

- **Discount vs. price cut**: a discount is a rare, documented one-off for a
  loyal client; a price cut sets a new, permanent price level (ch19).
- **Courtesy discount**: the invoice label that shows the real price, the
  discount, and the final amount, so a discount never becomes the new price
  (ch19).
- **First-Job Rule**: the first job with any new client must be profitable
  (ch19).
- **P = db ÷ D**: negotiating power equals the client's desire for the firm
  divided by the firm's desire for the client (ch22).
- **Give-gets**: concessions, and what to get in return for each, decided before
  negotiating (ch22).
- **Trial close**: an if-then question that tests whether a concession would end
  the negotiation (ch22).
- **Positive no**: saying yes to the underlying need and no to the specific
  request (ch22).
- **Rabbit**: a low bidder invited only to push another firm's price down
  (ch22).
- **White knight**: a senior person on the firm's side who arrives late and
  gives away margin (ch22).
- **Invoking policy**: stating a position as a predetermined decision rather
  than a preference. Policies beat preferences in negotiation (ch21).
- **Security bump**: a car dealer's one extra small ask after checking with the
  manager (ch21).
- **Pricing authority**: the right to set or accept a price, best held by
  whoever is least invested in the deal (ch21).

## Firm and mindset

- **Producer vs. marketer**: an efficiency-driven price taker vs. an
  innovation-driven price maker. A culture can hold only one (ch02).
- **60/45/20**: David C. Baker's producer targets. Utilization of 60% and
  payroll at or below 45% of AGI yield a 20% margin, a threshold that later
  becomes a ceiling (ch02).
- **AGI**: the firm's gross income net of pass-through costs (ch03).
- **AGI/FTE**: AGI per full-time equivalent employee, a firm-level predictor of
  profit that replaces timesheets. About $160k is respectable, a gap appears
  just above $200k, and value pricers cluster at $250k or more (ch03, ch23).
- **Capacity management**: treating about 10 to 12 client relationships as
  finite seats, segmented and yield-managed like an airline cabin (ch04).
- **Diagnose, prescribe, apply, reapply**: the four phases of an engagement. The
  first two are strategy, and the last tends to commodify (ch23).
- **PP = M × A × SE**: pricing performance equals motivation times ability times
  self-esteem (ch24).
- **Scarcity mindset**: the belief that a higher price is someone else's loss;
  the zero-sum fallacy (ch24).
