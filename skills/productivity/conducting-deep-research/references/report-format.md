# Research report

This structure is a default to adapt, not an exact template. Match the requested
medium and depth. When the user does not specify a format, produce a detailed
report with these sections:

1. **Executive answer:** direct answer, strongest findings, confidence, and the
   most important limitation.
2. **Scope and assumptions:** research question, goal, timeframe, geography,
   and inclusion boundaries.
3. **Evidence landscape:** what is established, emerging, disputed, or
   unsupported; note the strongest study types and evidence gaps.
4. **Findings:** organize by the questions or themes that matter to the user's
   goal, not by source. Cite claims inline and discuss contrary evidence.
5. **Research organizations and funding:** use a table when useful:
   organization, role, program or work, recipient, disclosed amount and dates,
   research focus, and primary source. Keep published research separate from
   funding activity.
6. **Implications for the goal:** state what each finding permits the reader to
   infer, what remains an inference, and any conditions that limit application.
7. **Uncertainty and open questions:** disagreements, missing data, possible
   conflicts of interest, and research that would change the conclusions.
8. **Sources:** complete citations with direct links and publication or
   provenance status.

Use exact dates where recency matters. Keep quotations short. Do not include
subagent, tool, or prompt history in the report. Put model or retrieval
limitations in the accompanying response.
