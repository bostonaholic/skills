## The six rules

| #   | Rule                                | Decision rule                                                                                                     |
| --- | ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Price the client, not the job       | Asked what the firm charges for a service, say it depends on the client. Quote nothing before the value is known. |
| 2   | Offer options                       | Three options per proposal (four at most), highest price last on the page. Never add a cheaper option.            |
| 3   | Anchor high                         | One option priced above where the client will buy, presented first.                                               |
| 4   | Say a price before you show a price | Call with the number before sending anything. Early objections are cheap.                                         |
| 5   | Master the value conversation       | Commit, metrics, value, then the $X to $Y range. No talk of solutions.                                            |
| 6   | Limit unpaid proposals to one page  | Option columns and prices, nothing else. Never sent unattended.                                                   |

## Value chain

Client, Value, Price, Cost, Solutions. The cost ceiling is about 60% of the
price. Solutions come last.

## Setting the range

| Input          | Benchmark                                                                                                                                   |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| $Y (top)       | A third of first-year profit (Enns's example); 20% of value, or a 5:1 return (Alan Weiss); half of first-year savings (large consultancies) |
| $X (bottom)    | The client's stated budget. The firm owns $Y; the client owns $X.                                                                           |
| Uncertainty    | Discount for performance risk; keep any guarantee hypothetical.                                                                             |
| Range too wide | Sell a paid diagnostic to narrow it.                                                                                                        |

## Option design

| Signal                                   | Response                                                                                  |
| ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The anchor sells 20% of the time or more | Raise the anchor and the middle option.                                                   |
| The low option always wins               | Close the gap between the low and middle options.                                         |
| The anchor won't sell anyway             | Push it higher to flatter the middle option.                                              |
| The client has in-house staff            | Home Depot structure: diagnostic, then strategy plus help, then full implementation.      |
| Price buyer or poker player is unclear   | Low option is a junior time block, middle option is this client's value, plus the anchor. |
| Nudging toward the middle option         | Use $19k rather than $20k; otherwise end prices in 000.                                   |
| Strategic work                           | Round numbers ending in three zeros, with no visible formula.                             |

## Choosing a pricing model

| Situation                             | Model                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Ongoing access to the principals      | Open access, at least $5k a month, with 10% or more off for a year paid in advance                                      |
| Large, sporadic implementation demand | Reserved capacity: hold the capacity and price the reservation                                                          |
| A retainer built as budget ÷ 12       | Refuse. Ask whether revenue certainty is overvalued, whether it suits both sides, and whether it is just easier to sell |
| The client won't pay a lump sum       | Lease: $50k becomes $3k × 36 = $108k, with a financed-amount clause                                                     |
| A single executive value buyer        | Satisfaction guarantee plus tip clause, offered per client, never as policy                                             |
| Many stakeholders                     | Tip clause                                                                                                              |
| An Agile shop                         | Fixed-price discovery, then sprints with milestone incentives                                                           |
| Points or hour banks on offer         | Decline; convert to dollars through the value conversation                                                              |

## Objection responses

| Objection                                 | Response                                                                                                    |
| ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Can't afford it                           | Offer payment terms. If heard low in the organization, go higher.                                           |
| Too risky                                 | Guarantee, or phase one paid up front with a money-back promise.                                            |
| Too much (willing, but wants to pay less) | Take out value. Never lower the price alone.                                                                |
| Wants a volume or long-term deal          | Trade price for a larger written commitment.                                                                |
| A new client wants a discount             | No. The First-Job Rule applies, and price buyers stay price buyers.                                         |
| A loyal client wants a discount           | Record it as a courtesy discount and ask the client to name the amount.                                     |
| Just send the proposal                    | Decline, citing policy: no proposals without the decision makers.                                           |
| One more small thing                      | Trial close: if the firm agrees to this, is there a deal? Then take the contract to the CFO before signing. |
| We need three bids                        | Invite them to get the bids; no hard feelings.                                                              |
| Nothing works                             | Walk away as a pause, and return in a few days with a new structure.                                        |

## Negotiation

- **P = db ÷ D**: power is the client's desire for the firm divided by the
  firm's desire for the client. The only lever is a credible willingness to
  walk. Don't overinvest in decks, travel, or teams.
- **Procurement after the closing conversation**: the firm has won. Start with
  no, out-wait them, and concede only give-gets decided in advance.
- **Procurement before the closing conversation**: price will decide; consider
  not bidding.
- **At the verbal yes**: neuter the final negotiators by asking the client
  whether you may refer procurement's call back to them.
- **Pricing authority**: the person least invested in the deal sets price.
  Usually not the creative lead or the new-business person, and often not the
  principal.

## Firm health thresholds

| Metric                             | Number                                                                     |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Client relationships               | About 10 to 12 at most; grow by engagement size, not count                 |
| MLOE                               | About 10% of the annual AGI target                                         |
| AGI/FTE                            | $160k respectable, a gap just above $200k, $250k or more for value pricers |
| Producer ceiling                   | 60% utilization and 45% payroll give a 20% margin                          |
| Price rise                         | A 10% increase adds about 50% to profit                                    |
| A pipeline deal called 99% certain | Assume about 40%                                                           |
| Car dealers' friends and family    | Charged about 25% more, for the extra service                              |

## Warning signs

- Hours estimated before anyone asks what the outcome is worth.
- A single-option proposal, line items, bios, or a free diagnosis.
- Prices that only ever travel by email.
- No one at the table who owns future value.
- Thinking about solutions in the middle of the value conversation.
- A retainer six months old or more in which one side feels shortchanged.
- A series of small asks, which means the firm is negotiating against itself.
- Being told the firm's bid is the highest, a hint that it may be the rabbit.
- A principal who keeps asking for status, a white knight in the making.
- Wondering how the price can be so high when the work took so little time, a
  sign the labor theory of value has taken over. Rebuild confidence through
  options: a pipeline larger than capacity, cash, and about a dozen clients.
